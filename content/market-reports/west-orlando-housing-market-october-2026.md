## The short version

September was the month rates took over the conversation. The Orlando metro median sale price was $399,610, almost exactly where it sat in August at $400,676 and a hair under September 2025 at $402,319. Sales slipped 6.2 percent to 2,325 closings. Inventory rose to 12,464 homes, or 5.3 months of supply, and the average home took 66 days to sell.

The number that changed everything else was the mortgage rate. The Orlando Regional REALTOR Association's September figure was 7.4 percent, up from 6.7 percent in August. Freddie Mac's weekly survey confirmed it on October 8: 7.40 percent on a 30 year fixed, the highest in nearly three years and a full point above the 6.30 percent of a year earlier.

In West Orlando that shows up as fewer buyers per listing, more price reductions, and a widening gap between homes priced for this market and homes priced for last spring. Priced right, they still sell. Priced for March, they sit through Thanksgiving.

These sources measure different things. A Zillow typical value is an estimate across every home, a Redfin or ORRA median comes from homes that actually closed, and the local sources run about a month behind ORRA. ## Windermere

Windermere is holding. Zillow's typical value is $707,471, up 0.1 percent over the year, and homes are going pending in about 40 days, the same pace as August. At the top of the market the picture is stronger than the typical value suggests: Redfin's July median sale price for the 34786 zip was $872,256, up 4.3 percent year over year, with 216 sales and 37 median days on market.

What that means in practice: the gated and lakefront segments are still moving because those buyers are less rate sensitive. The $600,000 to $900,000 band is where the 7.4 percent rate bites, and where a listing that misses its first two weeks starts chasing the market down. ## Winter Garden

Winter Garden had the strongest August of the four. Redfin's median sale price was $599,054, up 4.6 percent from a year earlier, on 178 closings, up 19 percent. Homes took 42 median days to sell, unchanged from last year, and sold at 97.9 percent of list. About 38 percent of listings took a price cut before selling, which is down almost 8 points from a year ago.

Zillow's typical value tells a flatter story at $566,535, down 1 percent, with homes pending in about 36 days. The two numbers together say the same thing: more homes are selling, the ones that sell are the well priced ones, and the historic district and golf cart zone still carry the premium. ## Horizon West

Redfin puts the Horizon West median sale price at $590,000 for August, flat year over year, on 386 sales, up 4.6 percent. The details:

- Median days on market: 42, unchanged from a year ago
- Sale to list ratio: 97.8 percent
- Sold above list: 8.1 percent
- Homes with a price drop: 40 percent
- Median price per square foot: $253

Forty percent of Horizon West listings cut their price before selling. That is the highest of the four markets and it is the builder effect: resale sellers here compete with new construction that answers a rate of 7.4 percent with rate buydowns and closing cost money rather than base price cuts. A resale seller who prices at the builder's sticker without matching the builder's incentives loses the buyer.

Village by village the spread is wide, from the mid $500s in Waterleigh and Lakeside Village to the high $600s in Hamlin and Village I. ## Clermont

Clermont is the one market with September closed data, and it shows the most movement. Redfin's median sale price was $441,790, down 4.0 percent from a year earlier, but 285 homes sold, up nearly 30 percent. Homes took 52 median days to sell, 17 days longer than last September. The sale to list ratio was 98.7 percent and 22.1 percent of homes sold above list, more than double last year's share.

That combination, lower median and far more sales with more bidding, means the action moved down the price ladder. First time and rate squeezed buyers who were shopping Winter Garden a year ago are buying in Clermont now. More in our [Clermont guide](/communities/clermont/).

Six months of supply is the textbook line for a balanced market. Orlando is at 5.3 and climbing, with more new listings arriving than closings leaving. Sellers no longer have the margin for a wrong price.

## Mortgage rates

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the 30 year fixed at 7.40 percent on October 8, up from 7.28 percent the week before and from 6.30 percent a year earlier. The 15 year fixed was 6.73 percent. On a $590,000 Horizon West purchase with 20 percent down, the move from 6.3 to 7.4 percent adds roughly $350 a month to the principal and interest payment.

That is why builder buydowns matter so much right now, and why a seller who offers a rate buydown or a closing cost credit often nets more than one who cuts the price by the same dollar amount. The buyer feels a monthly payment, not a list price.

## What to do with this

**If you are selling.** Price for the market you are in, not the one from spring. The first fourteen days carry more weight at 66 days on market than they did at 50. Offer the buyer something that lowers their payment, a buydown or a credit, before you reach for a price reduction. And sell with current, neighborhood level numbers: we pull the Stellar MLS comps for your street the week you list, not the month before.

**If you are buying.** Inventory at 5.3 months means choice, and 40 percent of Horizon West listings taking a price cut means leverage. Ask for the credit. Negotiate the buydown. In new construction, visit represented, because the builder's incentive is the whole game at this rate. If 7.4 percent is the problem, a two year buydown paid by the seller turns it into a 5.4 percent problem while you wait for a refinance window.

**If you are waiting.** Rates fell more than half a point between late 2024 and late 2025, then climbed over a point in a year. Timing the rate is a coin flip. Buying the right house at a negotiated price with a buydown is not.
