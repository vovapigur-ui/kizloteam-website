West Orlando Market, November 2026

Inventory hit a 2026 high and Orlando is one step from a balanced market. Prices slipped under last year, homes took longer to sell everywhere in West Orlando, and rates are back above 7.2 percent. Here is what the October 2026 numbers say about Windermere, Winter Garden, Horizon West and Clermont, and what to do about it before the end of the year.

By Vlad Kizlo, team lead · November 23, 2026

The short version

October was the month the market tipped toward buyers. The Orlando metro median sale price was $398,719, down from $399,610 in September and 2.9 percent under October 2025, when it was $410,543. Sales fell 6.0 percent to 2,185 closings. Inventory climbed to 12,762 homes, the highest point of 2026, which works out to 5.8 months of supply. Six months is the textbook line for a balanced market, and Orlando is now one step from it.

Rates did not help. The Orlando Regional REALTOR Association’s October figure was 7.18 percent, a little under September’s 7.28 percent, but Freddie Mac’s weekly survey has already moved back up: 7.28 percent on November 19, up from 7.11 percent the week before and a full point above the 6.26 percent of a year ago.

In West Orlando the story is the same in all four markets: homes are taking two to three weeks longer to sell than they did a year ago. Prices are mostly holding, but the seller who prices for last spring now waits past the holidays. The buyer who asks for a credit usually gets one.

These sources measure different things. A Zillow typical value is an estimate across every home, a Redfin or ORRA median comes from homes that actually closed, and Redfin’s figures are a rolling three months. Windermere

Windermere’s typical value is holding at $704,250, up 1.2 percent over the year, with homes going pending in about 42 days. Zillow counted 267 homes for sale in the town on October 31 against 52 new listings in the month, so a buyer has more to choose from than at any point this year.

The closed sale numbers tell a rougher story. Redfin’s median sale price for the 34786 zip was $679,652 for the three months ending October, down 24.9 percent from the same stretch a year ago, on 155 October closings. Homes took 68 median days to sell, 21 days longer than last year, and sold at 96.1 percent of list. About 35 percent of listings cut their price before selling.

Read the two together. The typical value says Windermere homes are worth about what they were. The sale median says the homes that actually closed this fall were smaller and cheaper ones, because the $1 million plus lakefront and gated segment slowed down most at 7 percent plus rates. If you own in that band, your home has not lost a quarter of its value, but it will take longer to find its buyer, and the first two weeks of pricing matter more than ever. The neighborhood breakdown is in our Windermere guide, with 2026 sale prices, HOA dues and school zones for Isleworth, Keene’s Pointe, Lakes of Windermere and Windermere Trails.

Winter Garden

Winter Garden posted the strongest price number of the four. Redfin’s median sale price was $593,087 for the three months ending October, up 8.4 percent from a year earlier, on 162 October closings, about even with last year. Homes sold at 97.1 percent of list and 7.6 percent went above asking.

The catch is time. Homes took 66 median days to sell, 18 days longer than a year ago. Zillow’s typical value is flatter at $564,335, down 0.2 percent, with homes pending in about 44 days, and it counted 727 homes for sale in the city on October 31. Nearly 40 percent of listings took a price cut before they sold, though that share is down more than 4 points from last year. The historic district and the golf cart zone still carry the premium; the sellers losing time are the ones in the newer subdivisions competing with builder incentives. More in our Winter Garden guide.

Horizon West

Redfin puts the Horizon West median sale price at $599,583 for the three months ending October, up 2.7 percent year over year, on 388 October closings, up 16.2 percent. The details:

  • Median days on market: 59, up 7 from a year ago
  • Sale to list ratio: 97.6 percent
  • Sold above list: 9.5 percent
  • Homes with a price drop: 37.7 percent, down 2 points
  • Median price per square foot: $249, down 2.2 percent

Horizon West is the one West Orlando market where sales volume is clearly up, and the price per square foot is clearly down. That is the builder effect: new construction is answering 7 percent rates with rate buydowns and closing cost money, and it is pulling buyers who would otherwise be shopping resale in Windermere or Winter Garden. A resale seller here still wins, but only by matching the builder’s incentive, not the builder’s sticker price.

Village by village the spread is wide, from the mid $500s in Waterleigh and Lakeside Village to the high $600s in Hamlin and Village I. Our Horizon West guide has the fee structure, schools and commute for each village, our Horizon West new construction guide covers the builders and what they are offering, and the Summerlake guide covers Summerlake in detail. For the Hamlin side, see Hamlin, Hamlin Reserve, Overlook at Hamlin and Storey Grove.

Clermont

Clermont shows the most movement, and it is still movement down the price ladder. Redfin’s median sale price was $434,698 for the three months ending October, down 5.5 percent from a year earlier, but 282 homes closed in October, up 16.4 percent. Homes took 61 median days to sell, 20 days longer than last year. The sale to list ratio was 98.3 percent and 17.8 percent of homes sold above list, up 11 points from a year ago.

That mix, a lower median with far more sales and more bidding, means the under $450,000 buyer is competing in Clermont while the rest of West Orlando waits. If you are selling a well kept three bedroom in Clermont, this is still your market. More in our Clermont guide.

The direction is the same either way. Inventory has risen every month this fall while sales have fallen, and at 5.8 months of supply the Orlando market is as balanced as it has been in years. Sellers no longer have the margin for a wrong price.

Mortgage rates

Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the 30 year fixed at 7.28 percent on November 19, up from 7.11 percent the week before and from 6.26 percent a year earlier. The 15 year fixed was 6.66 percent. On a $600,000 Horizon West purchase with 20 percent down, the gap between last year’s 6.26 percent and today’s 7.28 percent is roughly $325 a month in principal and interest.

That is why a seller who offers a rate buydown or a closing cost credit often nets more than one who cuts the price by the same dollar amount. The buyer feels a monthly payment, not a list price, and in Horizon West the builder next door is already making that offer.

What to do with this

If you are selling. Price for a 66 day market, not a 45 day one. Homes in every West Orlando market are taking two to three weeks longer than last year, so the first fourteen days carry the weight: that is when your listing gets its most showings and its only shot at multiple offers. Offer the buyer something that lowers their payment, a buydown or a credit, before you reach for a price reduction. And list with current, neighborhood level numbers: we pull the Stellar MLS comps for your street the week you list.

If you are buying. Inventory at a 2026 high means choice, and 35 to 40 percent of listings taking a price cut means leverage. Ask for the credit. Negotiate the buydown. In new construction, visit represented, because the builder’s incentive is the whole game at this rate. If 7.28 percent is the problem, a two year buydown paid by the seller turns it into a 5.28 percent problem while you wait for a refinance window.

If you are waiting. Rates fell more than half a point between late 2024 and late 2025, then climbed a full point in a year. Nobody has timed that well. A negotiated price with a seller paid buydown in a 5.8 month market is a better bet than a rate forecast.
