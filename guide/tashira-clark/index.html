Before you look at a single house

Florida looks simple from a listing photo. The cost of owning there is not.

Insurance, community fees, flood zones and property taxes all work differently there, and most of it never shows up in the asking price.

This guide puts the 2026 numbers for the Orlando area in one place, so you can plan a winter home or a full move with clear eyes. It is two pages, it is free, and there is no obligation.

What's inside the guide

  • Typical in Orange, Seminole and Osceola counties. Orlando sits more than 45 miles inland, and the age of the roof moves the price most.

  • A Community Development District fee in many newer communities. It is billed on the property tax bill, on top of the HOA, and listings rarely mention it.

  • Standard home insurance does not cover flooding. Most of inland Orlando is low-risk Zone X, where flood coverage is optional and inexpensive.

  • The seller's tax bill does not transfer. Taxes reset at the price you pay, so budget from the new assessed value.

  • Own and live in the home by January 1 and file by March 1 to lower your taxable value and cap yearly increases.

  • Florida closings run through title companies, and remote or mail-away closings are routine. Nobody has to fly down just to sign.

  • Schools are assigned by address, not by city or zip code, and they can change from one street to the next.

  • Storms weaken quickly over land. Newer homes meet strict wind codes, and a wind mitigation inspection can lower the premium.

  • Principal and interest, taxes at the new value, insurance, flood if needed, HOA and CDD. The guide shows where each number comes from.

The date that matters most

To claim Florida's homestead exemption for next year, you need to own and live in the home by January 1, then file by March 1.

A typical closing takes 30 to 45 days.
